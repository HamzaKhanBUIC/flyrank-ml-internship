Abstract
Published web content quietly loses search traffic over time. When an enterprise library holds 30,000 pages and an editorial team can only review 50 each month, guessing what to update is costly. Simple rules—like "refresh old pages with traffic"—fail, hitting only 24% precision on decaying content. In this project, we built a machine learning ranking engine trained on 30,000 real URLs across 32 enterprise clients. Using strict client-holdout validation and clean, leakage-free signals, our Random Forest model hits 74.0% Precision@50 (a 3.08× lift over the baseline rule) with an ROC-AUC of 0.785. We then packaged these predictions into an editorial action playbook with clear reason codes, turning content maintenance into a focused, high-ROI workflow.

01 Introduction & Problem Statement

In search engine optimization (SEO), traffic decline is rarely catastrophic overnight. It is a slow, quiet leak. Over months, algorithm updates, SERP layout changes, and fresh competitors steadily push top-3 rankings down into striking distance (ranks 4–10) or off page one entirely.

For an enterprise managing 30,000 URLs, a content team can realistically rewrite or update only ~50 pages each month. Most teams rely on basic rules like "refresh any page older than 6 months that gets traffic." Our audit shows this rule hits only a 24.0% accuracy rate on truly declining content—wasting 76% of editorial time on healthy pages while decaying revenue-drivers slip away unnoticed.

03 Methodology & Validation Design

To make sure our findings generalize to new client domains without memorization tricks, we follow three practical rules:

  • Grouped Client-Holdout Split: We split strictly by client_id (80% train clients, 20% sealed test clients). No client ever appears in both partitions.
  • Structured Missingness Handling: Content types that lack keyword volume (like syndication feeds) carry explicit missingness flags instead of blind zero-fills.
  • Adversarial Leakage Audit: We deliberately tried feeding the model future columns; models with leaky fields scored a fake 1.000 precision, proving our clean feature set is 100% honest.

05 Limitations & Honest Framing

Good engineering means being clear about what this model cannot do:

  • Observational, Not Causal: The model spots statistical patterns tied to past decay. It does not prove that making one edit (like adding 400 words) automatically restores traffic.
  • No Claims on Google's Algorithm: We do not reverse-engineer Google's internal code; we model site telemetry to help editors allocate time wisely.
  • Zero-Position Sentinel Limits: Pages with zero recorded search impressions need separate keyword retargeting.

06 Operational Content Action Playbook

We convert raw probabilities into an actionable queue using a composite Opportunity Score: $S = P(\text{decay}) \times \ln(1 + \text{impressions})$. Every recommendation includes an explainable reason code:

Action Code Trigger Condition Recommended Editorial Action
REFRESH_METADATA Striking rank (4–10) + Low CTR (<25%) Rewrite title tags & meta descriptions to win Page 1 clicks.
EXPAND_CONTENT High impressions + Low engagement Add clear comparison tables, FAQs, and expert insights.
STALE_CONTENT_REFRESH Age > 180d + High remaining volume Update outdated stats, refresh links, and verify facts.
ROUTINE_MONITOR Decay probability < 40% Healthy content requiring no immediate intervention.
